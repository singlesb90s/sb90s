Career Is Going Great. Dating Is Not.

You did the things. The degree, or the trade, or the self-taught skill. The job. The promotion, or the business, or the side project that turned into something real. You built a life you are proud of.

And then, somewhere in the middle of all that building, romance became a side quest. The one you kept meaning to get to. The one that is still sitting at level one while everything else is maxed out.

If that sounds familiar, you are not alone. There is a whole generation of us who did exactly this. We put our heads down, we built careers, we got good at our work, and we told ourselves the rest would sort itself out. It did not. It just waited, quietly, while the years went by.

The joke that goes around says it best. Maxed out your career stats, romance still level one. It is funny because it is true, and it is a little painful for the same reason.

The problem is not that you are too busy

People will tell you that you are just too busy, or too picky, or that you should put yourself out there more. That advice is useless, because it misses the point.

The point is that you spent years getting good at something, and the thing you got good at was work. Work has structure. It has goals, feedback, and a clear next step. Dating does not. Dating is a room full of strangers with no instructions, and nobody ever taught you how to do it.

So you did what any driven person does. You focused on the thing with a clear path. And now the clear path is done, and the unclear one is the one that matters most.

A room where romance is not a side quest

That is the whole idea behind SB90s.

Every SB90s event is a guided social for singles born between 1990 and 1999 in Waterloo Region. Think of it like a guided meditation. Instead of guiding your breath, the host guides the room to connect. Then you talk to singles. You meet and you mingle. That is it.

Here is what that means in practice. You show up to a room full of people who like the same things you do. A host walks the night through. Small groups, four to six people, so you actually talk to everyone instead of getting lost in a crowd. And the host introduces people, so nobody has to walk up to a stranger cold and figure out an opener.

No timed rotations. No scorecards. No speed dating energy. No pitch decks. No one is trying to sell you anything, and no one is trying to get funded by you. Just driven people, in a room, having real conversations.

You already know how to talk about your work

That is the funny part. You are not bad at talking to people. You talk to people about your work every day. You explain hard ideas, you pitch, you negotiate, you lead. You are good at this.

What you have not had is a room where the conversation is about everything else. What you care about. What you are like when you are not working. Who you actually are.

This is that room. The people in it are like you. They built something. They are driven, excited, and expressive about what they are doing. And they are here because they want to meet someone who gets that, not someone who is impressed by it.

You can tell a lot about a person by what they are building and why. It shows you what they care about, how they think, and what they are willing to work for. That is a much better window into someone than a dating profile.

Who it is for

Singles born between 1990 and 1999, in Waterloo Region, who are building something and want to meet someone who is too. A company, a side project, a freelance practice, a big idea that is still early. People who are looking for something real, a serious, long-term relationship, not another loop of matches that go nowhere.

The age range stays tight on purpose. You are meeting people from your own generation, not twenty years either side of you.

Friends are welcome too, as long as they are also single and born between 1990 and 1999. Come alone or bring a friend. Either way works.
